Sparsity Horse Race

Latent factors from the 51-signal zoo, at K = 3, 4 and 5, with sparse (spike-and-slab) and dense (normal) loadings, identified by a positive-lower-triangular restriction whose anchor signals are chosen on the training sample, priced against the 51-signal model in the submitted paper's two out-of-sample designs. Each cell is the Bayesian Fama–MacBeth fit at the posterior-mean risk prices; the small line beneath is the posterior width of the same statistic across risk-price draws, the precision claim.

Pricing chains: 2,500 draws, 1,000 burn-in. Factor-model chains: 5,000 sweeps, 2,000 burn-in, thinned to 1,000 draws. Built 2026-10-27 11:43.

Robert Hill. Companion note to In Search of Sparsity: Bayesian Sparse Factor Models and the Factor Zoo (Baba-Yara, Bondatti and Hill). A static version with every view expanded as tables: PDF.

Reading the table

  • Time split, prior Sharpe 1, out-of-sample RMSE. Every latent column beats the 51 signals on 60 anomaly portfolios, 49 industry portfolios, Chen and Zimmermann. The 51 signals beat every latent column on Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen. The 51-signal posterior interval is 2.3 to 5.8 times wider than the latent ones. Sparse versus dense loadings at the same K differ by at most 0.022, against a Monte Carlo spread of up to 0.100 in the benchmark column. Moving K from 3 to 5 moves the point fit by at most 0.044.
  • Asset split, prior Sharpe 1, out-of-sample RMSE. The 51 signals beat every latent column on 49 industry portfolios, Chen and Zimmermann, Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen. The 51-signal posterior interval is 2.9 to 4.3 times wider than the latent ones. Sparse versus dense loadings at the same K differ by at most 0.001, against a Monte Carlo spread of up to 0.002 in the benchmark column. Moving K from 3 to 5 moves the point fit by at most 0.001.
  • R². Out of sample the latent models' cross-sectional R² is close to zero on every panel in both designs, so their RMSE advantage in the time split is a smaller level error rather than explained dispersion; the 51-signal model's R² is strongly negative on the 60-anomaly and 49-industry time splits and positive on Chen–Zimmermann and Jensen. What was run

Three stages, all in the replication folder's MATLAB code, each gated on a go-ahead.

  • Anchors. For each K, one order-invariant Kaufmann–Schumacher run (the paper's sampler and prior, norm=1, utz=0) on the training months only, October 1973 to May 1995. Factors are ranked by exclusive loadings and the anchor of factor j is the signal with the largest median loading on j that is zero on every later factor. The size of the largest loading above the diagonal of the anchor block in the unrestricted fit says how much the triangular restriction fights the data.
  • Fits. Twelve chains: K in 3, 4, 5; loadings sparse (the paper's two-layer spike-and-slab at s0 = s1 = 0.1) or dense (a normal prior on every free loading, the sampler's prior=0 branch); fitted on the training months (for the time split) or the full sample (for the asset split). Identification is utz=1, norm=0 with no in-sampler label or sign switching, static factors, the anchors in rows 1 to K. Each fit starts from the training-sample median loadings in its own anchor order.
  • Factor panels. The factors handed to the pricing step are a fixed map of the standardised signals through the posterior-median loadings, F = Y S⁻¹ W (I + W' S⁻¹ W)⁻¹, built for all 519 months but with loadings and standardisation moments from the fit sample only. A factor is non-traded when more than half its loading mass is on the 17 non-traded signals; traded factors are appended to the test assets and price themselves, as in the submitted code.
  • Pricing. The unchanged oos_split_* and oos_* scripts, via run_oos_split and run_oos_ks10: Bayesian Fama–MacBeth with BHJ shrinkage and a spike-and-slab on the risk prices, prior Sharpe grid 1 to 3, both models run in the same loop. The 51-signal column is the same model in every run, so its spread across runs is the Monte Carlo noise ruler below.

Fixes applied before running

  • Degrees of freedom in the time split. The four split scripts handed the pricing sampler the full 519-month factor and signal matrices while the moments came from the 260 training months, so every posterior (both models) was computed as if twice the data had been seen. They now pass the training-period matrices. Widths in the time split are therefore wider than in the submitted tables, for both models.
  • Asset filter. The Chen–Zimmermann and Jensen split panels dropped one asset from each pair with |correlation| above 0.95 using the full sample; the filter now uses the training months. The panels therefore differ slightly from the submitted ones.
  • Look-ahead in the factors. The time-split factor panels come from models fitted on the training months only. The "paper K=3" column keeps the submitted paper's full-sample factor path (ftstar_3.mat) and is the only column with evaluation-period information in its factors.
  • Hand-tuned initialisation and signal order. Not used: the anchors and the starting loadings are read off the training-sample run, so nothing depends on row positions in the spreadsheet.

Caveats

  • One seed per fit. The loadings posterior is multimodal; a different start can land in a different basin, especially at K = 5.
  • Sign convention. The sampler fixes each factor's sign by the net sign of its squared loadings, not by the anchor, so an anchor loading can be negative in every draw. What matters is that "share of draws with anchor loading > 0" is 0 or 1; a value in between would mean the sign switched mid-chain. Pricing results are unaffected by the sign.
  • RMSE in the asset split is computed on demeaned pricing errors out of sample (as in the submitted Table 6); the time split uses raw errors in both samples (as in the submitted Tables 4 and 5). The two designs are therefore not on the same RMSE definition.
  • The 49-industry time split is undefined above prior Sharpe 1.5: the prior requires a Sharpe below the training assets' maximum of 2.0.
  • Static factors (philag=0). In earlier static runs the macro block loads on nothing at K = 3; it tends to appear as its own factor from K = 4.
  • Point fits are the plug-in at the posterior-mean risk prices (Reviewer 1's equation 1). The width is the posterior spread of the draw-by-draw statistic with the test-asset moments held fixed; it reflects uncertainty in the risk prices only.
